Does more agreement help?

A bot that buys any of these stocks when at least this many of the {{ L.checks|length }} rise checks agree, one trade per stock at a time.

Up to about 10 checks, more agreement changed nothing: every level lost about as much as buying on a random day. Above that, trades get rare. Hollow points have fewer than 100 trades, too few to tell skill from luck, and points with fewer than 10 are left off. With this rule a trade has to win about half the time just to break even after costs.

How this was tested
  • Every trading day for each of these stocks from {{ combos.start }} is a possible entry. A check is true on a day using that day's close and earlier data only.
  • Rise checks: {{ L.checks|map(attribute='label')|join('; ') }}.
  • The bot holds one trade per stock at a time. A trade picked on 2021–2024 must close before {{ combos.split }}.
  • Combinations are screened on every matching day, then the top ones are rerun with the one-trade-at-a-time rule. A combination that only adds checks that change almost nothing is skipped.
  • With thousands of combinations tried, some look great on the picking period by luck alone. That's why the picks are checked on later data. A few dozen trades still leave a wide margin for luck.
  • Research on trading rules finds the same pattern: rules that look profitable in one period rarely survive a fair test on another once the number of rules tried is accounted for (Sullivan, Timmermann and White, 1999; Bajgrowicz and Scaillet, 2012).
